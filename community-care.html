A wash, a doctor, a ride

  • Tulsa, showers and laundry: Tulsa Day Center, 415 W Archer St, 918-583-5588. Their 2025 report says people can shower, do laundry, and use the clothing room. They also help with vital papers and keep a kennel for pets. Call and ask what is open today.
  • Tulsa, a clinic: Morton Comprehensive Health, 1334 N Lansing Ave, 918-587-2171. Medical, dental, vision, and behavioral health. Call for an appointment and ask the cost. Mon–Fri 8:30am–5:30pm, Sat 9–1. Thursday stays open until 7pm.
  • Oklahoma City, a clinic: Variety Care, 405-632-6688. Call for an appointment or the nurse line. Ask the cost. Offices are around the city, not only at the main desk.
  • Tulsa, the bus: MetroLink. Riders 18 and under ride free. Adults 65 and older, riders with a disability, and people on Medicare can get a reduced fare at the Denver Ave Station. This is not a free pass for every adult.
  • A phone of your own: If you are on SNAP, SoonerCare, or a low income, you may qualify for Lifeline. Apply at lifelinesupport.org. Do not pay a stranger who says they will sign you up.
  • A young person: Under 18 and need to get safe or get home, call 1-800-786-2929.

Work for a place to stay, Oklahoma

This is not emergency shelter and not a Temple bed. Raven's Nest does not trade a room for labor yet. Call and get the terms before traveling.

Country Gardens: about 105 acres in north-central Oklahoma, about 3 miles from a town of 1,100 and about 50 miles west of Tulsa. They list work-exchange housing and host WWOOF visitors with a written agreement. Call before you go.
More short farm stays are on wwoofusa.org, which costs $45 a year. One is a small house in Norman, not an off-grid commune, and it is best to ask about the rules and the room before driving.
