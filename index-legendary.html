The Power of Dependency

The power held by wealthy institutions and corporate monopolies is not magic. It is built on dependency.

Imagine a king holding a staff. The staff does not contain supernatural power. It represents the laws, systems, resources, and dependencies that allow one person to command everyone beneath them.

If you want to end that king's power, you do not have to become a new king and seize the staff for yourself. You take away what gives the staff meaning. You build alternatives.

Today, that staff is our dependence on centralized systems for the necessities of life: food, water, energy, housing, transportation, work, and everyday goods. When people have no choice but to rely on systems they cannot influence, those systems have leverage over them.

But when communities can produce and share more of what they need, that leverage begins to disappear. That is the work Raven's Nest is meant to do.

The Vision

Picture a community where people work together to secure land, grow food, collect and protect water, develop renewable energy, learn trades, build practical housing, and care for one another.

A community where people grow food together. Where knowledge is shared instead of hoarded. Where people teach each other how to build, repair, grow, create, and survive.

A sanctuary where compassion isn't just a nice word. Where compassion becomes infrastructure.

Animals are treated with respect and compassion. Our elders, our disabled neighbors, our veterans, our families—the people society too often treats as disposable—are treated with dignity. A community should not measure a person's worth by how much profit can be extracted from them.

Raven's Nest is meant to be a home for peaceful people from many paths. Teachers, craftspeople, gardeners, healers, artists, priests, priestesses, elders, students, and neighbors all have something to offer.

We don't need everyone to believe the same things to recognize that hunger is wrong, homelessness is wrong, loneliness is wrong, exploitation is wrong, and community is worth building.
